Camping and Leave No Trace

Pick a safe site, store food, handle fire and stoves, and leave the place better than you found it.

Choosing a site

Good campsites are found, not made. Where there is an established site, use it.

  • Durable ground. Pitch on bare soil, gravel, rock or dry grass, never on fragile plants or moss.
  • Away from water. Camp at least 200 feet (about 70 adult steps) from lakes and streams. That protects the bank and the water, and keeps you clear of a rising creek.
  • Look up. Check for dead branches and leaning trees (widow-makers) above your tent before you pitch it.
  • Look down. Avoid dips and dry streambeds, where rain collects and flash floods run. A spot a little higher than its surroundings drains better.
  • Think about wind and weather. Use natural shelter from the wind, but stay off exposed ridges and summits in thunderstorm season.

The seven Leave No Trace principles

The principles below come from the Leave No Trace Center for Outdoor Ethics. The explanations are ours.

  1. Plan ahead and prepare. Know the rules and the weather, travel in small groups, and repackage food to cut down on rubbish.
  2. Travel and camp on durable surfaces. Stay on the trail, even through mud, and walk single file. Off trail, spread out on rock, gravel or dry grass.
  3. Dispose of waste properly. Pack out everything you pack in, including food scraps and toilet paper where required. Bury human waste in a cathole (see below).
  4. Leave what you find. Leave rocks, plants, antlers and old artefacts where they are, and don't build structures or dig trenches.
  5. Minimise campfire impacts. Use a stove for cooking. If you have a fire, use an existing fire ring, keep it small, and burn it down to ash.
  6. Respect wildlife. Watch from a distance, and never feed animals. Store food and rubbish where they cannot get it.
  7. Be considerate of other visitors. Keep noise down, give way on the trail, and camp out of sight of others where you can.

Campfires

First check that fires are allowed. Fire restrictions change with the season and can change overnight. If fires are allowed:

  1. Use an existing fire ring, clear of overhanging branches, tents and dry grass, and well away from tree roots.
  2. Have a full bucket of water and a shovel at the fire before you light it.
  3. Gather only dead, downed wood no thicker than your wrist. Never cut live trees or break branches.
  4. Build from small to large: tinder, then kindling no thicker than a pencil, then fuel wood. Light it from the side the wind is coming from.
  5. Keep it small, and never leave it unattended, even for a minute.
  6. To put it out: drown it with water, stir the ashes, and drown it again. Then feel with the back of your hand. It is out only when everything is cold to the touch.

Camp stoves

  • Cook outdoors on flat, stable ground, away from tents and anything that burns. Never use a stove, lantern or heater inside a tent: it can burn, and the carbon monoxide it makes can kill.
  • Let the stove cool completely, and move well away from any flame, before you change a canister or refuel.
  • Keep spare fuel away from the stove. Store it outside your tent.
  • An adult should supervise younger scouts, and long hair and loose sleeves should be kept clear.

Food and wildlife

An animal that gets human food once will keep coming back, and in bear country that animal may have to be destroyed. Keep food and anything with a smell away from where you sleep. That includes toothpaste, sunscreen, rubbish and the clothes you cooked in.

  • Use bear-resistant canisters or lockers where they are provided or required. Check the rules for your area before you go.
  • Where canisters are not required, hang food in a bag from a high branch, well out of reach of the ground and the trunk, or use a hard-sided container away from camp.
  • In bear country, keep sleeping, cooking and food storage well apart, around 200 feet from each other.
  • Never keep food in your tent, not even a snack.

Dishes and toilets

Washing up
Scrape plates clean and pack out the scraps. Wash 200 feet from water with a little biodegradable soap, strain out food bits, and scatter the grey water widely.
Catholes
Use a toilet where one exists. Otherwise, dig a hole 6 to 8 inches (15–20 cm) deep and at least 200 feet from water, camp and trails. Fill it in and disguise it afterwards. Pack out toilet paper where required.
Hand washing
Wash with soap and water or use hand sanitiser after the toilet and before cooking. Most stomach upsets on trips come from hands, not water.

Before you leave

Walk the whole site in a line, side by side, picking up anything that wasn't there when you arrived, including small things like twist ties and bits of foil. Check that the fire is cold. Leave the site so that the next group can't tell you were there.
