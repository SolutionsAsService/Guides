About this handbook

Compass & Trail is a free, open handbook of outdoor skills for scouts, their leaders and anyone learning to find their way outdoors.

What it is

It is an independent project, published as open source under the MIT licence. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by any scouting organisation, and it does not replace your organisation's own handbook, requirements or safety rules. Where they differ, follow your organisation and your leaders.

Outdoor skills are learned by doing. Use this handbook to prepare and to refresh what you have learned, and practise every skill outside with a qualified leader.

The original guide

This repository began as a single structured guide: Surveying and Mapping with a Lensatic Compass, written as JSON so that it could be read by people and programs alike. It is the seed of Chapter 1 and is kept, unchanged, in docs/. You can also download the original file.

When we turned it into a chapter, we corrected and expanded it:

  • Reading a bearing. The original said to turn the dial to meet the needle. On a lensatic compass the dial turns by itself, and you read the bearing under the fixed index line. The bezel ring is for setting a bearing to follow.
  • Triangulation. To find your own position, you draw lines from the landmarks along their back bearings, not the bearings you took. The chapter explains back bearings and the triangle of error.
  • Declination. The rule for converting between magnetic and true bearings is now spelled out, with a calculator.
  • Slopes. A lensatic compass has no clinometer, so the chapter now uses a separate one, with a worked height example.
  • Interference. We added safe distances from metal from the US Army manual, how to calibrate a pace count, and a worked field notebook for mapping an area.

Contributing

Every chapter is a plain HTML file in _src/pages/, and the chapter list and skills checks live in _src/chapters.json. Edit them, run python _src/build.py, and open a pull request on GitHub. Corrections with a source are especially welcome.
